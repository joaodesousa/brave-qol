## Design Context

### Users

Brave users who want small quality-of-life features they recognize from other
browsers without changing their browser, creating an account, or sending work
to a hosted service. They value quick, obvious tools that stay out of the way.

### Brand Personality

Playful, precise, independent. The voice is direct, cheerful, and confident:
useful software with no hype, no telemetry, and no paid-tier theatre.

### Aesthetic Direction

Playful utility grounded in the existing brave-qol identity. Use warm paper
(`#F3F1EC`) and near-black ink (`#14171A`) with teal (`#00968C`) and orange
(`#FF5A1E`), plus light tints derived from those accents. Pair chunky rounded
display type and sticker-like labels with the brand's monospace wordmark.
Use oversized symbols, light rotation, and crisp hard shadows for energy.
Product screenshots should remain authentic, sharp, and legible evidence of
the extension, not decorative mockups. Avoid generic SaaS gradients,
glassmorphism, neon cyber styling, childish illustration, excessive cards, and
inflated marketing language. Do not use em dashes in copy.

### Design Principles

1. Show the real Brave experience prominently and keep important UI legible.
2. Make one clear promise per composition; supporting copy stays concise.
3. Use asymmetry, scale, oversized symbols, and playful tension with control.
4. Reserve teal and orange for navigation, emphasis, and brand continuity.
5. Communicate privacy and simplicity through restraint rather than badges or
   unsupported claims.
